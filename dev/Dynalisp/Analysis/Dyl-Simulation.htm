Simulation

The developer docs of the simulation of a 2D detail. What it does and how to use it are in its User Guide page.

How It Is Split

The Lisp keeps what it already does: the materials and their user copies, the climates, the units, the window. The plugin reads the geometry from the database of the drawing, much faster than Lisp, and the calculation lives in a library with no reference to AutoCAD (Core, netstandard2.0), compiled into the plugin and tested alone with dotnet test Installer\Simulation\Tests, on any computer: the Simulation workflow runs the tests and builds the plugin on each push that changes Installer\Simulation. The plugin has no WPF, so it builds on Linux too (dotnet build Installer\Simulation -c Release, which copies both DLLs to Contents\Resources; commit them with the source).

The Data In The Drawing

The zones carry the extended data of the application DYNALISP, as a region carries its material (see Assign Material), written with Remote>>Dyl:MaterialTagPut:

ObjectKeys
A simulation zone (a closed curve)Role=SimulationZone, Grid=Coarse|Medium|Fine (*sim:grids*: the target size of the cells, 10, 5 or 2 mm, converted to drawing units with Remote>>Dyl:MaterialUnitMetres). Its Material is taken off; an outline that has one is refused.
A climate zone (a closed curve)Role=Climate, Climate=<id>, the id of a row of Climates.csv (cl-Interior).
A regionMaterial=<id> on a hatch or a closed curve; a hatch without it is read as a region of no material.

The zone of the session is kept in *sim:zone* (a global of the drawing), the text of the last check or run in *sim:last*. Dyl:SimZoneCurrent gives the zone of the session while it exists, else the only zone of the drawing.

The Climates

Res\Climates.csv, read with Dyl:UserFile (Dyl:SimClimates, *sim:climdefs* when the file has no row) and written to its user copy (Dyl:SimClimatesWrite): Id;Name;Temperature;Humidity;Rs;RsCondensation, in SI (°C, %, m²·K/W), each value empty when not known. The defaults have the surface resistances of ISO 6946 and ISO 13788, and the interior 21 °C and 35 % (the indoor conditions behind NBC 9.25.1.2., Chown and Mukhopadhyaya, NRCC-47656, 2005); the exterior −23 °C, the January 2.5 % design temperature of Montréal (NBC 2020, Division B, Appendix C, Table C-2). A temperature or a humidity is given only with its source. A climate of a zone without a temperature stops Play (Dyl:SimNoTemp).

The window shows them in the units of the drawing (sim.imp, Remote>>Dyl:MaterialImperial-p: °F and R), each row of the list holding the values shown, the values of the file and the values shown when the window opened (Dyl:SimClimRow); a value shown and not changed is saved as it was (Dyl:SimClimOf), never rounded by opening the window. A climate added gets the id cl-<name>, made unique.

The Window

Dyl:SimulationDialog shows Res\Dyl-Simulation.xaml in a loop: the buttons that act on the drawing close it with their number, the Lisp does the work, then shows it again on the values it got back (the climates edited, the climate selected cl.sel, the grid).

The Check, Step By Step

  1. Dyl:SimCheck reads the grid of the zone, the materials (Dyl:SimMaterials: the ids of every material of Remote>>Dyl:MaterialBoth, and of those that have a thermal conductivity or a fixed RSI, columns 5 and 7) and the ids of the climates, and calls (Dyl:SimulationRead zone size known data climates).
  2. The plugin (Reader.Read) reads the objects of the space of the zone whose extents touch it: the hatches (their loops, Hatch.GetLoopAt, arcs every 10°, splines and ellipses in 96 points, in the plane of the hatch), and the closed curves that carry a Material or a Climate. The draw order of the space (DrawOrderTable.GetFullDrawOrder) is the order of each region.
  3. The library (Detail.Read) builds the grid (Grid.Lines): its lines go through every vertex of the zone and of the regions, lines closer than a quarter of the target size merged, each interval divided to the target size, two neighbours within a factor 2. Each cell gets what its center is in, each row scanned once per shape (even-odd, an island of a hatch outside it): the region of a material in front, else the climate zone in front, else a hole; a cell outside the zone is left out. The holes are grouped by the cells that share a side.
  4. The plugin classes the regions that have cells in the zone (no material, a material not known, a material without data, a climate not known), shows the grid, the holes, those regions and the zone as transient graphics (Show.Detail, TransientManager, kept by drawing until Dyl:SimulationClear, the next check or the drawing closed), and returns the counts: (cells nx ny regions nomaterial unknown nodata holes overlaps badclimates climatecells grid (material ...) (climate ...)), or a text when the zone cannot be read. The reading and the classing are Check.Of, shared with the run.
  5. Dyl:SimReport makes the text of what was found, the problems first, printed on the command line and kept for the window.

Play, Step By Step

  1. Dyl:SimPlay gives the plugin what Dyl:SimCheck gives it, but the materials with data as texts "id|conductivity|resistance|permeability|permeance" (Dyl:SimMaterials) and the climates as "id|temperature|humidity|rs|rscondensation" (Dyl:SimClimTexts), all in SI, with the units of the labels and the metres of a drawing unit: (Dyl:SimulationRun zone size known mats climates imperial metres).
  2. The plugin checks the zone (Check.Of). With a problem, or a climate of the zone without a temperature, it shows the check as Dyl:SimulationRead and returns (check nil): nothing is solved.
  3. Each region gets its conductivity: that of its material, else its fixed RSI as an equivalent conductivity, its thickness the smaller side of its bounds over its RSI (Thermal.Equivalent: an air space drawn as a band). Each climate region gets its temperature, humidity and two surface resistances.
  4. Thermal.Solve (Core) solves the steady heat, one unknown per cell of a material at its center (finite volumes): between two cells of a material, the conductance of their face is its length over the two half cells in series (the harmonic mean, exact for layers in series); between a cell and a climate, the half cell in series with the surface resistance, to the temperature of the climate; every other face is adiabatic (the cut of the zone, a hole). The cells of a material that touch no climate through other materials are left out (Isolated), so the system stays positive definite. It is solved by conjugate gradients preconditioned by a modified incomplete Cholesky factorization (MIC(0), τ 0.97), to a relative residual of 10−10: 360 000 cells with steel through insulation in about 400 iterations, a couple of seconds. It runs twice when the climates have a condensation resistance other than their surface resistance: with Rs for the heat flow, with Rs condensation for the surface temperatures (ISO 13788).
  5. Summary.Of takes the interior (the climate of the highest temperature that touches the detail) and the exterior (the lowest): the heat flow from the interior Q (W/m), L2D = Q / (Ti − Te), the length of the interior surface and the effective RSI = length × (Ti − Te) / Q, from the heat run; the coldest interior surface (the temperature of the face, Tc − q × Rs), its place and material, fRsi, and the length of interior surface under the dew point, from the condensation run. The dew point is that of ISO 13788 (Annex E: the saturation pressure over water at 0 °C and above, over ice below), Psychro.
  6. When every material of the detail has a vapour permeability δ (else its fixed permeance times the smaller side of its bounds, Vapour.Equivalent: a membrane drawn as a thin band) and every climate of the zone a relative humidity, Vapour.Solve (Core) solves the steady diffusion of vapour on the same grid, with the temperatures of the heat run with the Surface resistance of the climates (ISO 13788, clause 6): the conductance of a face is its length over d/δ of the two half cells; a climate face holds the cell to the vapour pressure of the climate (its humidity times the saturation pressure at its temperature, no surface vapour resistance, as ISO 13788). A cell above the saturation pressure at its temperature (Psychro.Saturation) is held at it and the system solved again; a cell held that takes in less than it gives out is let go; until nothing changes (Glaser in two dimensions, a primal-dual active set). Between the solves, the cells held and their neighbours are relaxed alone (Vapour.Relax, projected Gauss-Seidel), which moves the edge of a band held by one cell a sweep at the cost of the band only; the solves are warm-started, at a residual of 10−6 while the cells held change and 10−10 for the last one. What a cell held condenses is what comes in less what goes out (ng/(s·m)); Summary.Add adds them up into g/(m·day), with the area and the materials condensing and the highest relative humidity elsewhere. Otherwise the result names the materials without vapour data (NoVapour) and the climates without a humidity (NoHumidity).
  7. Show.Field draws the cells of a material in 32 colours (fewer while there would be more than 60 000 rectangles), merged along each row, blue (exterior) through white to red (interior), the cells not solved grey (with Result.Humidity, the relative humidity of each cell instead, a sequential scale from white to dark blue, ColorBrewer Blues); the cells condensing filled in magenta; the dew point line by marching squares on the centers of the cells (Contour.Segments, a saddle split by its mean); the interior faces under it and a circle at the coldest one, in magenta; the zone; a legend at its right (DBText transient). It keeps the run of the drawing for the probe and the views (Show.Result: both heat flows, the summary, the vapour, the zone).
  8. The plugin returns (check result), the result a list of pairs ("Ti", "Tsi", "fRsi", "Dew", "Rsi", "L2D"... see Plugin.cs), a value not known left out. Dyl:SimResult writes it in the units of the drawing (°F, R, Btu/(h·ft·°F) in an imperial drawing).
  9. Dyl:SimProbe calls (Dyl:SimulationProbe point) for each point picked: (temperature material climate [rh pv condensing]) of the cell there (Grid.CellAt) in the last run of the drawing. Humidity and Temperature of the prompt after Play call (Dyl:SimulationView "Humidity"), which draws the last run again with that field (Show.View).

Keep And Report, Step By Step

  1. Dyl:SimPlay keeps the last run it solved in *sim:lastrun*: (zone grid res climates materials text), for the session.
  2. Dyl:SimKeep asks the interval of the isotherms (*sim:step*, in °C or °F as shown) and calls (Dyl:SimulationKeep step height hatch), the text height 2.5 mm and the hatch spacing 3 mm on paper (Remote>>Dyl:MaterialDrawScale, Remote>>Dyl:MaterialUnitMetres), in one undo mark.
  3. The plugin (Keep.Draw) takes the run of the drawing (Show.Last): for each level of the interval between the lowest and the highest temperature, the segments of marching squares (Contour.Segments) joined into lines where they share an end (Contour.Chains), each a lightweight polyline (closed when its ends meet), the longest labelled with a DBText; the same for the dew point; one ANSI37 hatch of a rectangle loop per run of cells condensing, not associative. All in the current space, on the current layer. It returns their enames by kind.
  4. Dyl:SimKeep puts each one on the layer of its key (Remote>>Dyl:LayerKeyApply), which makes the layer from the key when the drawing has none.
  5. Dyl:SimReportWrite asks the file (getfiled, <drawing> - Simulation.txt beside the drawing), writes the lines of Dyl:SimReportLines (the climates and the materials of the check, items 13 and 12, with their data from the files, the text of the results, the methods) in plain ASCII, and opens it with the program of Windows (Shell.Application).

Diagnostics

  • DynALisp section: DynALisp.Simulation.dll not loaded (a problem in AutoCAD 2015 and later).
  • Drawing section (dg:dwg-simulation): the climates of the file and a note for those without a temperature (Play refuses them), the simulation zones and the climate zones of the drawing, and a problem for a climate zone of a climate the file no longer has.

Limits

  • Only the objects of the space of the zone are read: no block, xref, region or solid, no object of another layout.
  • A curve that is not in the plane of the drawing is read by its X and Y; a hatch is read in its own plane, its points then given in X and Y.
  • A Polyline2d with arcs is read by its vertices (its arcs as chords); a lightweight polyline keeps its arcs.
  • A grid of more than a million cells is refused (Detail.MaxCells); one of more than 1500 lines on an axis is checked but not drawn.
  • A cell takes the material at its center: an oblique edge or an arc is followed in steps of a cell (no conductivity averaged by area yet).
  • An air space is simulated as a solid of its RSI across the smaller side of its bounds: an air space drawn otherwise than as a band gets a wrong thickness.
  • Steady only: the vapour is the method of Glaser, by diffusion alone (no liquid water, no air leakage, no storage), for the conditions given; a transient run is the last step of the plan.
  • A membrane is a region of its own, drawn as a thin band that the grid follows (its permeance across the smaller side of its bounds); a membrane drawn as a line is not read.
  • Vapour is computed only when every material of the detail has vapour data: a material without it is not taken as tight or open.
  • ψ needs the walls without the bridge: L2D is given, ψ is not computed yet.
  • Validated by tests (a wall of layers against its resistance in series, the heat in against the heat out, a steel stud; the vapour of a wall against its linear pressure, and the condensation of a wall against Glaser), not yet against the reference cases of ISO 10211 nor THERM.
