Windows

The developer docs of the windows of DynALisp: the plugin that shows them, the window files, and how a DCL window is moved to it. What the user sees is in its User Guide page.

Why WPF

A DCL window cannot be resized, has no real list or tab, looks like Windows 95 and can only be pictured by running AutoCAD. A WPF window looks like the rest of Windows, and its picture is drawn from its file, without AutoCAD, as the pictures of the ribbon are. DynALisp already needs .NET for the ribbon drop-downs and the clean startup; AutoCAD LT and AutoCAD for Mac were never supported (no full AutoLISP, no ActiveX). The cost: each window is rewritten once. DynALisp 2.0 runs in AutoCAD 2025 and later (1.x, on release/1.x, keeps the .NET Framework build for AutoCAD 2015 to 2024).

How It Works

  1. The Lisp calls (Dyl:WinShow file vals): file a .xaml of Modules\Dynalisp\Res (or a full path), vals the values of its keys, as set_tile takes them. Dyl:WinDocs reads the file and its sub-windows for their docs pages (dyl:Win.Docs) and adds what each one opens (Dyl:DocsPage, Dyl:DocsTarget: the website, in the language of DynALisp).
  2. Dyl:Window (Bridge.cs) reads the values into a Session and shows the window over AutoCAD, modal (Application.ShowModalWindow, owned by the main window of AutoCAD, without the size and the place AutoCAD keeps for its own windows: persistSizeAndPosition false, Fit.cs places it); a window with tabs and an event is modeless (see Tabs).
  3. Session.Load reads the file with the WPF reader (the dyl: prefix, clr-namespace:DynALisp.Windows with no assembly, is the plugin itself) and merges into its resources the styles of Theme.xaml and the Dyl.* brushes of the theme of AutoCAD (Theme.Brushes: the grey blue of its palettes in the dark theme, COLORTHEME 0, its light greys else, its blue for what is picked; dark under the DynALisp Neutral interface of Dyl-Settings (DynalispDisplayOn and DynalispInterface, read by Bridge.NeutralInterface through Theme.NeutralOf at each window; the Assistant palette asks Theme.Neutral again when it is shown or the mouse comes over it), the same colours in neutral greys, a fifth darker, its texts as light; the pictures of the docs (Shots) keep the grey blue; a CheckBox is drawn as a switch, as in the settings of Windows 11); a dark window gets the dark title bar of Windows. Its root must be a Window; a file that does not read, or is missing, makes Dyl:Window return nil, the reason on the command line.
  4. View finds every control by its name (x:Name, the key), gives it its value, and puts its value back each time it changes; the rules are then checked again (Refresh). The value win/title, when the Lisp gives it, is the title of the window in place of the one of its file, then dropped (the Preset Groups window on the groups of the materials: Material Groups).
  5. A button closes the window with its number, opens a sub-window over it on the same values (its Cancel puts them back), turns every checkbox on or off, or opens the docs. When the window closes, Dyl:Window returns (status (key . value) ...).

The Window File

A window file is plain XAML, with no code: a control is named by its key, and the dyl:Win.* properties (Engine\Win.cs) say what it does.

A Sketch Of A Choice

A choice whose items draw differently (the ends and the corners of Quick Draw) shows a sketch of the item picked, drawn in the window file itself, with no code: a Border of the Dyl.Sketch style holding a Viewbox, a Grid of the size of the drawing in it, and in the grid one Canvas per item, shown by its rule (dyl:Win.VisibleBy="men=Round"); a rule on a path inside shows what another value changes (the joint of the corners, mjo=Interrupted). The paths take the styles of Theme.xaml, the classes of the sketches of the docs (docs.css): Dyl.Sketch.Outline the border, Dyl.Sketch.Center the line drawn (dashed, in the accent), Dyl.Sketch.Ghost what is not drawn, Dyl.Sketch.Dim, Dyl.Sketch.Point, Dyl.Sketch.HatchA and HatchB (lines at 45 and -45 degrees, the brushes Dyl.HatchA and Dyl.HatchB that Theme.Brushes makes in the muted ink of the theme), Dyl.Sketch.Text and Dyl.Sketch.Ask (the text in place of a sketch, for Ask when drawn). A dash of StrokeDashArray is in widths of its line, not in units. The coordinates are those of the svg of the docs page, so the two are kept alike.

The Preview Of A Hatch

HatchTile draws a hatch from the line families of its pattern, read from its .pat file by Pat.Read (angle, origin, offset and dashes of each family; kept until the file is written again, so a pattern rebuilt by Dyl-RebuildPat is read again). It follows the Presets And Materials convention on previews: the hatch at its size on paper, 1:1 on the screen (a WPF unit is 1/96 in).

  • Its Spec, given by the Lisp, is one band or the bands of an assembly side by side, divided by *, the fields of a band by ?: P?width?pattern?file?ppu?angle?scale?type a hatch (type: Drafting, the default, or Model, the type of its hatch preset), F?width a solid fill, S?width a space, L?0 a line between two bands, X?width a material missing (crossed out, as a pattern no file defines).
  • ppu is the inches on paper of one unit of the pattern: the scale of the material (divided by 25.4 for a metric pattern), divided by the drawing scale scale unless the preset keeps its size on paper (Dyl:QuickDrawGalleryBand: annotative or adaptive scaling, so a Drafting material; scale is then 1). The drawing scale is Remote>>Dyl:MaterialDrawScale (CANNOSCALEVALUE).
  • A Drafting band is always drawn 1:1, never zoomed: every Drafting hatch is seen at the same scale, in a list as in a preview, so their scales can be compared and adjusted (a pattern larger than the tile shows a part of it). When fewer than three repeats of a Model pattern (Pat.Repeat: the widest spacing between its lines; not its dash periods, since a stipple reads as a texture at any size) fit in the height of the tile, it zooms out to the next step of 1, 2, 2.5, 4, 5, 8, 10, 16, 20, 25, 40, 50, 100... that shows them, one zoom for every Model band of the tile, and writes 1: and scale times the zoom in its bottom right corner. A family whose lines would be closer than 0.8 unit, or a tile of more than 20000 segments, is drawn as a grey fill.
  • An assembly – AssemblyTile (Engine\Assembly.cs, a HatchTile) draws the bands of an assembly one under the other, as a wall in plan: its line from left to right, the first layer at the top, each layer ending at the right as it ends (Square, Open, Extended, Round, Angled: across its own thickness when it has its own band settings, else across the whole band), its own border in Accent, the layers selected tinted. Its Spec adds to each band, divided by ~: its ends, extension, end angle, own, border, selected, its own line (Edge: on its border nearer to the line of the band), load-bearing. Its Line, the key of a justification (Center, Edge, Structural), draws the line drawn, dashed in Accent: in the middle of the band, on the border of its first layer, or in the middle of its load-bearing layers together; the Angled ends of the band turn around it. Given the inches of a drawing unit and the drawing scale (Paper), it draws the wall at the step of the scale that fits it in the tile, its hatches at the same scale (their size against the thickness as drawn); else it fills the tile, its hatches as HatchTile. It follows a list of rows (see Lists Of Rows).
  • Every segment of a band is one StreamGeometry, frozen, drawn in one call: a gallery of 50 tiles draws in a few milliseconds. Its colors are the brushes of the theme (Ink, Fill, LabelBack: Dyl.Text, Dyl.Input, Dyl.Surface in the gallery).

Preset Windows

A window that lists presets and edits the one shown (Quick Draw, Materials, Hatch Presets) is a preset window: its list is a ListBox named lst, and the Lisp gives its presets as a table, pst/rows. The window is then run by PresetEditor (Engine\Presets.cs), which does in the window everything the preset dialog of the Lisp engine did with a call for each click: the list (filter, sort, Show group, Set default), the presets selected (a value changed is given to all of them), New, Copy, Delete, Reset Selected, Reset All (Delete and Reset All ask first), and the aliases of the command of the preset shown. Nothing calls the Lisp back: it gives the presets, gets them back as edited, checks them again and saves them.

  • Tables – a value given as a list of lists, ("pst/rows" ("qd-Brick" "Section" "Brick" ...) ...), is a table (Session.Rows); the tables the window changes come back the same way, after the values: (status (key . value) ... ("pst/rows" row ...) ("pst/als" ...)). In a sample of Shots, a line +pst/rows=qd-Brick<TAB>Section<TAB>Brick... adds a row.
  • The values – pst/tiles names the control of each column ("nam:2,grp:1,mth:4,hbr:28:inv": inv a checkbox showing the inverse of its column, up a text saved in capitals). When the preset shown changes, the editor fills them (quietly: no rule runs for each one), then the rules are checked once. A value changed is written in the column of every preset selected, its list item updated in place.
  • The material – mat lists the items of pst/mats (the materials of a preset tool, the hatch presets of the Materials window), those of the group of the preset shown, or all of them when pst/matfree is "1" (the group changed then leaves it as it is). With mgr/list (the groups of the materials, (matgroups . T) of the Lisp), the list mgr of the window, a ComboBox that is not a column, picks the group mat lists: it shows the group of the material of the preset shown, another group picked gives the presets selected its first material when theirs is not in it, and the group of the preset no longer changes the list; mpv previews it. With pst/matview (the column of the view of a preset), an item of pst/mats holds its Surface too (its preview and pattern after those of its Cut), used while that column is Surface, for the preview and pst/solid. pst/matnoun is what the items are called in the messages ("material", "hatch preset"), and the status under the name says what a preset is called (pst/noun without its plural: Default material).
  • The rows – the columns of the presets file, then the columns the Lisp adds for the window only (the text beside an open button, the preview of an assembly), read only: the Lisp cuts them off before saving (Remote>>Dyl:PresetWinCut). The editor sets pst/solid to "1" when the material of the preset shown is SOLID, for the rules. pst/follow ("38:44,45,46") and its table pst/follow/rows ((value v44 v45 v46) ...) give columns of the window the values of the item picked in the list of a column (the width, the preview and the description of the assembly preset of a Quick Draw preset), as soon as it is picked.
  • Presets that are hatches – with pst/hatch ("3,4,6,9,10,63": the columns of the pattern, scale, angle, annotative, adaptive scaling and type of a hatch preset), the preview of each preset in the list and in mpv is drawn from its own columns as they are edited, not from a material: no pattern a space, SOLID a fill, else its pattern from the file pst/pats gives it ((PATTERN file metric) ...), its scale in inches on paper divided by the drawing scale pst/ds, unless it keeps its size on paper (annotative or adaptive scaling, never a Model hatch), as Remote>>Dyl:MaterialBand does (the Hatch Presets window).
  • Families – with pst/family ("13,14": the columns of the name of the family of a preset and of its short description), the presets are the variants of families (the assemblies: B6a to B6e are the family B6). The list shows each family once, its first variant by name (numbers sorted as numbers) standing for it, its number of variants in Count of the item; its filter matches the family, its description or any of its variants. Selecting a family selects all its variants (pst.sel), pst.many counts the families. The variant shown is picked in var (a ComboBox: its name, then the values of pst/famlabel, "9:min,11:STC": a column, the word written before it, min minutes as the code writes them, a value the same as that of the column before it left out); vnw adds a copy of it named after the family with the next free letter, vdl deletes a custom variant of a family that has others. A value of a family column (its group, fam its name, unique among the families, its description, and the columns of pst/famcols, "6,16-39") goes to every variant of the families selected; any other to the variant shown, or to every variant while several families are selected. New and Copy add a family of one variant, Delete removes every variant of the custom families selected. The window says the noun of its presets (pst/noun), never family, and its count (pcn) only says No match.
  • The aliases – pst/als, ((ALIAS COMMAND) ...), shown as chips (als). An alias is refused, with its reason in ams, when it has other characters than letters, digits, - and _, is the command itself, starts with DYL-, LM- or PST-, is an alias already, or is a name of pst/taken (the commands defined, the aliases of acad.pgp: Remote>>Dyl:PresetWinTaken). A custom preset without id gets cu-<Name> with its first alias, as Remote>>Dyl:PresetCamel.
  • A window on the presets – a button with dyl:Win.Open and dyl:Win.Columns="out:8,bco:9" that is not on a list of rows opens that window over the preset window on the preset shown, its columns as the values of those keys, as a window on the rows; its OK gives them to every preset selected (the Border lines and Dividing lines of an assembly, columns the Lisp adds).
  • The checks – each value is checked as it is typed (dyl:Win.Check, a unique and not empty name, a group, a material that exists): a value not valid is not kept, its message is shown in error, and the list and the buttons that close the window are refused until it is fixed (Cancel always closes).
  • The Lisp side – Remote>>Dyl:PresetWin (Remote\Dyl-Presets.lsp) shows the window of a preset tool whose spec has (window . "Dyl-QuickDraw.xaml"), gives it its values (Remote>>Dyl:PresetWinVals), reads them back (Remote>>Dyl:PresetWinGet), checks the presets changed with the check of the App and the new aliases with getcname (Remote>>Dyl:PresetWinCheck: the first one not valid opens the window again on its preset, its message shown), and runs the code of an open button (Remote>>Dyl:PresetDlgCode) before showing it again. A tool without window keeps the DCL dialog (Quick Shape).

Lists Of Rows

A window that edits a list of rows (the layers of an assembly, the groups of the presets) has a ListBox with dyl:Win.Rows="1". The window is then run by RowsEditor (Engine\Rows.cs): the rows are added, edited, removed and ordered in the window, with no call to the Lisp, which gives them, gets them back as edited and checks them again (Assembly Presets, Preset Groups).

  • The rows – for the list k: k/rows the rows (a table), k/new one row, what the fields show while the list is empty and the row Add starts from, k/defs the rows of Reset, k.sel the rows selected ("0 3"). It gives back k/rows and k.sel. The list shows RowItem: {Binding [n]} the text of column n (the label of its item when a field with a list shows it), {Binding V[n]} its value, for the DataTriggers of its ItemTemplate.
  • The fields – dyl:Win.Column="n": the control shows column n of the row shown (the first one selected): a ComboBox the value stored for its item (key/vals), a TextBox its text, a CheckBox "1" / "0". A field changed goes to every row selected that its rules hold for (the dyl:Win.EnabledBy of the field and of the panels holding it, read on that row), their other columns kept. dyl:Win.Unique="message": no two rows with the same text (any case), greyed out while several rows are selected, a row added gets (2). dyl:Win.Follows="lp/widths": the field takes the item of that list (one for each item of the field lp) when lp changes, if it was empty or the item of its former value. dyl:Win.Exclusive="1" on a checkbox: on in one row at most, cleared in the others when it is checked (the first row selected keeps it); "lock": while one row has it, the field is greyed out on the others, and a checkbox of the list binds IsEnabled="{Binding Free[n]}" (RowItem.Free: true while no other row has column n on). dyl:Win.ListBy="lg" on a list field: it lists only its items whose value in key/by (one for each item, a group) is the value of the field lg in the row shown, every item while it is empty; lg changed gives the rows the first item of the new group when their item is not in it (the Material list of a layer of an assembly, by its Group).
  • Checkboxes in the rows – a CheckBox of the ItemTemplate with dyl:Win.Toggle="n" sets column n of its own row when it is clicked ("1" / "0", RowsEditor.Toggled), the row not selected for it, and clears it in the other rows when the field of that column is dyl:Win.Exclusive; it shows the column with IsChecked="{Binding V[n], Mode=OneWay, Converter={x:Static dyl:On.Converter}}" (On of Engine\Win.cs: "1" is checked). It is a small square box, Style="{DynamicResource Dyl.Check}", lighter than a switch on every row. The Load-bearing, Variable thickness and Low permeance columns of the layers of an assembly.
  • The buttons – dyl:Win.Do: add (a row of the fields after the rows selected), remove, up, down (one row selected), flip (the rows the other way), reset (k/defs), each one greyed out when it can do nothing.
  • A window on the rows – a button with dyl:Win.Open and dyl:Win.Columns="men:7,mex:8" opens that window over this one on the row shown: its columns as the values of those keys (the index of the value for a list with key/vals), so its controls edit them; its OK gives them to every row selected that its rules hold for, its Cancel keeps them. It is greyed out while no row is selected. The other values it needs (its lists) come from the Lisp with the rows (the Band Settings window of a layer of an assembly).
  • The preview – an AssemblyTile whose Rows is the key of the list is given its Spec on each change of the rows or the selection, from its Columns (kind, preset, thickness, band on, ends, extension, angle, border, hatch aligned to, load-bearing), Specs (the preview of each item of the field of the preset, its width replaced by the thickness; a ppu w0.0005 times the thickness), Ends (the ends of the band) and Paper, and given the value of its Line each time it changes.
  • The summary – dyl:Win.Summary on a TextBlock or a GroupBox (its header): {count}, {count:lk=L|line|lines} (the rows a rule holds for, a rule without |, with their noun), {sum:2:lk!=L} (the total of a column), {sel| (# selected)} (shown when two or more are selected), {per:1:2:lp/rsi} (the total over the rows of the value of the item each one picks in the field of column 1, from the list lp/rsi/list, aligned with the items of that field: "a b", a per unit of column 2 plus b, 4 significant digits; the rows whose item is "" left out) and {miss:1:lp/rsi|, 1 layer unknown|, # layers unknown} (those rows left out, "" when there are none): the thermal resistance and the mass of an assembly. A row can be two items, a framing and its cavity: {per:1:2:lp/rsi:44:45:lfm/rsi:p} adds the item of column 44 (none: "") with its share in percent in column 45, from lfm/rsi/list, in parallel (p: 100 / (share / framing + rest / cavity), the isothermal planes of the building code) or by area (a, a mass), and {miss:1:lp/rsi:44:45:lfm/rsi|...} counts the rows either one has no data for. {over:spec@lfm=@lfm!=|Continuous layers #|unknown} adds up only the rows a rule holds for, "" when it holds for none or the rule after a second @ holds for no row (the continuous layers, those without a framing, once a layer has one); {ratio:first:spec@llp|Low permeance ratio #|unknown} (last: from the other end) divides the rows from that end to the first row the rule holds for, included, by the others, 2 decimals (the low permeance ratio); the third text when a row has no data. A TextBlock whose summary is empty takes no room. dyl:Win.SummaryEmpty while the list is empty. The same value for one row is {Binding P[1:2:lp/rsi|–]} (with a framing P[1:2:lp/rsi:44:45:lfm/rsi:p|–]) in the template of the list (RowItem.P), the text after | when its item has none.
  • The rows of a preset – in a preset window, dyl:Win.RowsIn="n" on the list makes its rows those of the column n of the preset shown (the layers of an assembly preset), a row on each line, its columns divided by tabs: they are loaded each time another preset is shown (PresetEditor.Show calls RowsEditor.Load, none selected but on the first showing, k.sel) and each change is given back to that column of the preset shown only (PresetEditor.PutShown); k/rows is not given. The preset editor sets pst.many to "1" while several presets are selected, for the rule that greys the rows out. The Lisp turns the column back into its own column of the file (wget of Remote>>Dyl:PresetWinGet) and checks the whole preset (rowcheck of Remote>>Dyl:PresetWinCheck).
  • The checks – a field is checked as it is typed (dyl:Win.Check, dyl:Win.Unique): a value not valid is not kept. On OK, every row is checked for every field its rules hold for, the first one not valid selected with its message; dyl:Win.Check="rows" on the list keeps one row at least.

The Startup Suite Window

The Startup Suite window (Res\Dyl-Suite.xaml) lists every command of the modules, its list a ListBox named lst and its commands given as the table ste/rows. The window is then run by SuiteEditor (Engine\Suite.cs, its columns at its top), which does in the window what the DCL dialog did with a call for each click: the Module, Group and Show lists, the search as it is typed, the sort (a child after its main command), the folding of the children (the arrow of a line, a double-click, Fold/Unfold), On/Off (the box of a line, SuiteItem.On; a dynamic child follows its main command), the aliases of the command selected (ste/als, refused with the names of ste/taken), and opening the docs and the Lisp files (the targets the Lisp gives in each row). It gives back ste/rows, ste/als and the state of its list (ste.mod, ste.grp, ste.opens, ste.sel), so the Lisp opens it again as it was.

A Button (not an open button) whose key the Lisp gives a value takes it as its label: the answers of a question (Res\Dyl-Ask.xaml, Dyl:AskIn).

Lists And The Child Picker

A plain list (ListBox) gets its items from key/list and gives back the index picked. Its items may hold columns divided by a tab, shown by its template with {Binding Converter={x:Static dyl:Cell.Converter}, ConverterParameter=n} (Cell of Engine\Win.cs, the column n from 0), as the tabs of a DCL list_box. dyl:Win.Close="n" on the list makes an item double-clicked close the window with n, as its Run or OK button.

A plain list lets its user work in the window, as the actions of a DCL window did, with no call to the Lisp:

  • Several items – with SelectionMode="Extended", it gives back the indexes selected divided by spaces ("0 3 5", "" for none), as get_tile of a DCL list_box with multiple_select, and takes them the same way. A rule tests it with lst!= (an item selected).
  • The boxes it fills – dyl:Win.Fills="var:0,val:1": the item picked puts its column 0 in the box var and its column 1 in val (a text box, a text, or a list whose items are the column divided by commas). Columns not shown can hold what the boxes show (the description of a system variable, the definition of a word).
  • A search – dyl:Win.Filter="lst:0,2" on a text box lists only the items of lst whose columns 0 or 2 hold every word typed, accents, ligatures and case ignored (Session.Fold, as dic:fold); "lst:4;ind:5" searches the column 5 in place of 4 while the checkbox ind is on. The list still gives the indexes in all its items. A text with dyl:Win.CountOf="lst" and dyl:Win.CountText="{}{0} of {1} words" counts them (listed, all).
  • A list that follows another one – dyl:Win.ItemsBy="rev" on a list or a text: its items (its text) are the row i of the table key/by the Lisp gives while the item i of the list rev (a list or a drop-down) is the first one selected, its own key/list (its text) while none is; a list keeps the indexes selected. The sheets of the Revisions window show so what each one has of the revision picked, with no call to the Lisp. A drop-down is followed each time the rules are read again (a preset window sets it quietly), and a text that follows it can be a rule: the units of the pattern picked under the scale of the Hatch Presets window, and a hidden pfl ("1": a .pat file has it) that enables the open button of that file.
  • A list that closes – dyl:Win.Close="n" on a ComboBox: an item picked closes the window with n, for a choice the Lisp must act on (another profile, another dictionary), as the action of a DCL popup_list.

A window that asks for a name (a new, copied or renamed profile) is Res\Dyl-Name.xaml: its title, the label of its box (lbl), the name proposed (nam) and why the name typed was refused (error). The Lisp checks the name on OK and shows it again with its message (sv:dlgname of System Variables).

The picker of the child commands (Dyl:ChildPicker, the main command of a file of presets: Make Layers, Layer Filters, Standard Grid...) is one window for all of them, Res\Dyl-ChildPicker.xaml: the Lisp gives its title (win/title), the children turned on (aliases TAB name), the hint of the caller (hnt) and the page of the main command as the target of its Open Docs button (docs/child, the window file naming dyl:Win.Docs="child", a page no file has).

Tabs: The Manager

A window of several tools is the window of the tool of its tab, under a row of tabs: the Manager (Remote\Dyl-Manager.lsp). Each tab is its own window file, run by its own Lisp, since nothing calls the Lisp back while a window is open. With the values of the window of the tab shown, Dyl:WinShow gives tab/list (the titles), tab (the tab shown), tab/title (the title of the window) and tab/place (the name its place is kept under, whatever the tab). Engine\Tabs.cs then:

  • puts the window content in a TabHost, the row of tabs docked on top (the tab shown in the Dyl.Heading ink, underlined in Dyl.Accent, the others in Dyl.Muted); Fit.cs scrolls the content only, adds the height of the row to the window and keeps it at the place of tab/place: one size and one place for every tab, never made larger or smaller for the tab shown (its least size a constant, TabsLeastW and TabsLeastH, not that of the tab: a tab that needs more room scrolls), always resizable, so the window of the next tab is drawn exactly over the one left. Only the first tab ever shown opens at the size of its content. Only the first window of a session gets it: a sub-window (dyl:Win.Open) never does;
  • ends the window with TabHost.Status (90) when another tab is picked (a click, Ctrl+Tab, Ctrl+Shift+Tab, Ctrl+Page Down / Up), its values checked first as OK does (View.CloseWith), its place saved (TabHost.Leaving, Fit.cs), the tab picked in tab;
  • shows it modeless, so the user draws while it is open, when the Lisp gives it an event (tab/event, a Lisp expression: (Remote>>Dyl:ManagerEvent key tab)). Dyl:Window shows it with Application.ShowModelessWindow (Modeless of Engine\Tabs.cs) and returns nil at once: the Lisp of the tab ends as on Cancel, and the window holds what is edited. A button that needs the Lisp (another tab, Save, OK, Draw, an open button that closes the window: every status but 0) is taken by View.Intercept: the window stays open, disabled (EnableWindow), and the event is sent to the command line of the current drawing (SendStringToExecute, after ^C^C when a command is running, Bridge.cs). The Lisp of the tab runs again as if it had just shown its window: its Dyl:Window on the same window file gets the button and the values of the window (Modeless.Request), as from a modal window. A tool can ask for them before it makes the values of its window, (Dyl:WindowTake file) (Modeless.Take, through Dyl:WinTaken of dynalisp.mnl): the same list as Dyl:Window gives, or nil when that window waits for nothing; the preset windows do (Remote>>Dyl:PresetWin), so a click never makes every value of a window the Lisp will not show. The window it shows next (the same tab again, the next tab, a message) replaces it at the same place, without the animation of Windows (DWMWA_TRANSITIONS_FORCEDISABLED), the new one shown hidden and not activated until it is drawn (Reveal.cs, below), then activated as the window left closes, so the screen goes from one tab to the next without a blank window or a change of focus in between; a window without tabs (a sub-window of the Lisp, Materials from Quick Draw) is modal over it. (Dyl:WindowDone), called once the event is done, closes the window when the Lisp did not show it again (OK, Save, an error), and gives it back when the Lisp never took its button. A window whose event never runs (no drawing open, AutoCAD busy) is given back after 30 seconds. Cancel and the close box close it at once: nothing is saved. A Dyl:Window of the Manager while it is open shows the tab asked for in it (TabHost.PickTab: the tab left is saved), or brings it to the front.

The preset engine treats status 90 as Save (Remote>>Dyl:PresetWin), and the Manager shows the tab picked.

A Tab In .NET

A tab moved to .NET is shown by a Lisp function of its own, with no Lisp in between: (Dyl:AsmWindow file ctx) (PresetTabs.cs) for the Assembly Presets. It makes the values of the window with DynALisp.Core.dll (AsmTab, see Core: the same values as the preset engine gave), shows it modal, or modeless with the tabs of the Manager, and reads back, checks (the window shown again on its message) and saves the assemblies. The Lisp (Dyl:QuickDrawAsmDialog) gives in ctx only what the Lisp alone has: contents, the system variables (vars), the names of the layer keys (keys), the key of the border and the assembly of the Quick Draw preset (border, select), the default Show group (shw), the units of the patterns (pats, of Dyl:AsmTabPats), the assemblies when the Lisp reads them (rows) and the values of Dyl:WinShow (vals: the tabs and the Open Docs buttons). It gets (status ("tab" . "i") ("id" . id) ("written" . "1") ("counts" (GROUP . n) ...)): nil when shown modeless; -1 to be given the assemblies read by the Lisp (an older file made of a Quick Draw preset); -2 when the materials cannot be read; 0 Cancel; 1 OK and 2 Save, the assembly selected; 8 to show the Preset Groups window (still in Lisp), then call it again with resume and the groups renamed (renames); 90 another tab, saved. Between two calls it keeps the rows it sent and the assemblies being edited, so a click of the Manager takes the button (Modeless.Take) before any value is made, and the window comes back as it was left after the Preset Groups window; the sort and the assembly shown last are kept for the session, and Set default of Show is saved by it (setenv of DynalispPresetShowQuickDrawAssembly). Only that class uses DynALisp.Core.dll, referenced by the project and never copied with it: the one AutoCAD loaded is found by Plugin.Resolve, so the other windows open without it. The picture of the docs is drawn from Samples\Dyl-Manager.txt: the Quick Draw window with the tabs.

Shown Once Drawn

No window is seen before it is drawn. Shown at once, a window is white for a moment, then placed and sized (Fit.cs), its lists given their heights and its previews drawn over its next frames. Reveal.Attach (Engine\Reveal.cs) hides it by Windows (cloaked, DWMWA_CLOAK: drawn, but not on the screen) as soon as it has a handle (SourceInitialized), and shows it once its layout has settled: its first frame (ContentRendered), then 80 ms without a layout (LayoutUpdated), then nothing left for the dispatcher (ApplicationIdle). It is shown 1.5 s after it has its handle whatever happens, and as before on a Windows that refuses the cloak. Session.Show does it for every window file and its sub-windows, the Manager for each of its tabs (with the window of the tab left closed in the moment the next one is shown), the question of a preset window for itself; a window made in C# outside Session.Show calls it before it is shown. Its first frame alone was not enough: the light tab of Quick Shape was whole at its first frame, the tabs of many fields and previews (Quick Draw, Materials, Hatch Presets, Assembly Presets) were not.

Size, Resizing And Place

A window file says nothing of its size on the screen: it is written at the size of its content (a StackPanel of a fixed Width, its lists of a fixed Height), as its picture shows it. Shown over AutoCAD (Session.FitToScreen, set by Bridge.cs; the pictures of Shots never are), it goes through Engine\Fit.cs:

  • Its content is put in a ScrollViewer: what does not fit the window scrolls.
  • What grows – every ListBox, Gallery or TextBox with AcceptsReturn of a Height of 80 or more, and every ListBox without a Height (it gets its MinHeight, or 240: a list without a height would be as tall as all its items), but those with a MaxHeight (the chips of the aliases). dyl:Win.Grow="1" makes any element grow, "0" keeps a list as it is. A list fills the room its panel gives it (the last child of a DockPanel, a row of a Grid as tall as the column beside it).
  • A window with something that grows can be resized (ResizeMode.CanResize, its minimize button removed: it is modal): by its borders, maximized and snapped by Windows. The height it has more or less than its content goes to its lists, shared by the lists one above the other in proportion to their height (a list beside them gets it all), down to 100 (a Gallery 200); the width it has more goes to what holds them and has a fixed width: the Width of their panels (and of a list itself) and the column of a fixed width of a Grid they are in, or the ColumnDefinition of that Grid with dyl:Win.Grow="1", shared by those side by side. A column or a panel whose MinWidth is below its Width gets narrower too, down to that MinWidth, when the window is: the width missing is taken from those side by side, in proportion to what each can give (the columns of the assemblies and of the layers of the Assembly Presets window); a column of a fixed width with a MinWidth that holds no list (its Parameters) gets narrower the same way, never wider. It is never narrower than its content with those at their MinWidth (MinWidth of the window) nor shorter than its content with its lists at their least (MinHeight). The lists follow each layout (LayoutUpdated: a row shown or hidden by a rule too), only when the view or the content changed.
  • Its size – it opens at the size of its content, at most the work area of the screen of the window that opened it (AutoCAD, or the window under a sub-window), centered on that window and kept inside the screen (SourceInitialized, before it is shown). A window with nothing that grows keeps SizeToContent, its MaxWidth and MaxHeight the work area.
  • Its place – a window that can be resized opens where it was left, at its size, maximized when it was (kept inside the screen nearest to it, at least its least size): IPlaces, kept by Bridge.cs in one user setting, DynalispWindowPlaces (Dyl-Suite:left,top,width,height,maximized|..., device-independent units), written when the window closes. A setting of this computer (its screens): never saved with the settings (*dyl:setslocal*, LocalSettings of the setup), removed by the uninstall with every Dynalisp* setting. Delete it to open every window at its own size again.

dotnet run -c Release -- -fit in Installer\Windows\Shots shows each window of Shots.csv on the screen as AutoCAD does, at its least size, larger, maximized and opened again, and prints the sizes of the window, its view and its lists (SCROLLS when its content does not fit). The Windows workflow runs it after the pictures, on the screen of its runner.

Moving A DCL Window

  1. Write its .xaml in the same Res folder, one file per dialog, its controls named by the keys of the DCL tiles, so the Lisp keeps its names. What the actions only greyed out or turned off (mode_tile, Dyl:MoreTile) becomes a rule of the file; a sub-window opened by an action becomes a dyl:Win.Open file on the same values.
  2. In the Lisp, the set_tile / start_list of the fill become a function giving the values (Dyl:SetWinVals), the get_tile of the OK a function reading them back (Dyl:SetWinGet); load_dialog, new_dialog, action_tile and start_dialog become one Dyl:WinShow.
  3. Delete the .dcl and the DCL functions (no copy is kept: every AutoCAD DynALisp runs in loads the plugin). Add a row and sample values for its picture to Shots.csv, and update its developer docs page.
  4. Try it in AutoCAD 2025 or 2026 (the .NET 8 DLL) and in AutoCAD 2027 (the .NET 10 DLL of Net10) when you have both.

The Pictures Of The Docs

Installer\Windows\Shots draws each window of Shots.csv (the window file, its sample values in Samples\, the picture its docs page names) with the engine of the plugin, at twice its size, under a plain title bar, in the light theme and in the dark one (<Page>-window-dark.png, shown by the website in dark mode). Known Limitations

  • No callback to the Lisp while a window is open (see above).
  • The theme is read when a window opens (COLORTHEME: 0 dark, 1 light; the Interface of Dyl-Settings): a window open while the theme changes keeps its own.
  • The windows are in English, as the DCL windows were.
  • 64-bit only: a 32-bit AutoCAD (2015 to 2019 had one) does not load the plugin.
